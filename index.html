Learn · Decimal Coefficients

Subtract Decimal Coefficients

Example: 1.8y − 0.9y. The part-whole bar shows 1.8y as 18 tenths. Subtracting 0.9y removes 9 tenths; the shaded part that remains is 0.9y.

Part-Whole Bar Model · Solved
1.8y = 18 tenthsSubtract 0.9y = 9 tenths
Remaining: 9 tenthsSubtracted: 9 tenths
18 tenths − 9 tenths = 9 tenths
Written Method · Solved
1.8y − 0.9y
= 18/10y − 9/10y
= 9/10y
= 0.9y

From the bar model, 1.8y − 0.9y = 0.9y.

Learn · Fractional Coefficients

Subtract Fractions with a Part-Whole Bar

Example: ⅔b − ¼b. Divide b into twelve equal sections. The diagram shows the 8/12b part, the 3/12b being subtracted, and the 5/12b that remains.

Method 1 · Part-Whole Bar Model
Whole: b = 12 twelfths2/3b = 8/12b
Remaining: 5/12bSubtracted: 3/12bOther 4/12b
Method 2 · Rewrite the Fractions
2/3b − 1/4b
= 8/12b − 3/12b
= 5/12b

The least common denominator of 2/3 and 1/4 is 12. Rewrite both coefficients with denominator 12, then subtract.

Learn · Decimal Coefficients

Subtracting a Negative Decimal

Example: 4.6m − (−1.2m). Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its opposite.

Written Method · Solved
4.6m − (−1.2m)
= 4.6m + 1.2m
= 5.8m
Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its opposite.

Learn · Fractional Coefficients

Subtracting a Negative Fraction

Example: ¾n − (−⅝n). Rewrite subtraction of a negative as addition. Rename ¾n in eighths, then combine like terms.

Written Method · Solved
3/4n − (−5/8n)
= 3/4n + 5/8n
= 6/8n + 5/8n
= 11/8n
= 1 3/8n
